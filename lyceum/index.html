Can we win it?

Qualification is set membership, not judgment. Your team either holds the categories a solicitation requires, or it does not — and the ones it is missing can be named by code. We answer that first, in seconds, so a pursuit that was never winnable never reaches your proposal team.

Can we handle it?

Capacity is the second question, and today it silently answers the first: firms bid what they had time to read. When the check is instant, every solicitation gets screened — not just the ones that arrived on a slow week. You see the whole field and choose where the hours go.

How it works

From solicitation
to first full draft.

Five stages, in order. The first three are deterministic — the same input gives the same answer every time. Only the fourth writes prose, and nothing is ever submitted without your review.

  1. 01
    Solicitation inManaged by Lyceum

    Send the RFP, RFQ or letter-of-interest request. We read out the required categories, page limit, DBE/HUB goal, self-performance minimum, evaluation weights and the submittal deadline. Nobody on your staff learns new software.

  2. 02
    Go / no-go gateDeterministic · never AI

    Seven checks, pass or fail, each with its reason. Eligibility is arithmetic and set membership — a language model here would only add a chance of being wrong about something verifiable.

  3. 03
    Team matched to scopeDeterministic

    Approved categories are matched as sets, evaluated as of the submittal deadline rather than today. A certification that lapses before you submit is a gap, and it is named as one. A denied category surfaces as a risk instead of a silent hole.

  4. 04
    Library assembledDeterministic

    Bios, precertification appendices, past performance, QA/QC language, certification statements, the organization and time-commitment tables. Most of a response already exists inside your firm — it gets assembled, not rewritten.

  5. 05
    Narrative draftedGenerated · then reviewed

    Project understanding, tailored approach, why this team — written against the agency's scoring weights and page budget. It arrives as a draft for your PM. You edit, seal, sign and submit.

What's automated, and what never will be

The line runs through
the middle of the document.

We measured it against two real submissions — a TxDOT district letter of interest and a long-form MPO corridor-study RFQ. The proportions held.

Your library 70–80% of the page count

Looked up and assembled, word for word, from material your firm already owns and has already had approved. No invention, nothing to fact-check.

The gate Pass / fail · no prose

Pure arithmetic. Categories compared as sets, so 8.2.1 can never be satisfied by 18.2.1 — the kind of near-miss that costs a submission and is invisible on the page.

The narrative 20–30% of the page count

Drafted, constrained to the page budget, and handed to a human who knows the project. This is the only part a model writes, and the only part that always gets read before it goes out.
